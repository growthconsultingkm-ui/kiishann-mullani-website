Local businesses — clinics, salons, restaurants, gyms, repair services, boutiques, tutors — often get marketing advice designed for brands with national budgets. Much of it doesn't fit. A local business doesn't need to reach everyone. It needs to be the obvious choice for the right people within a few kilometres.

Here's a practical, low-complexity strategy built around three jobs: **be easy to find, be easy to trust, be easy to choose.**

## 1. Be easy to find

When someone nearby needs what you offer, where do they look? Usually: a map search, a general web search, a recommendation from a friend or a quick look on social media.

- **Complete your Google Business Profile.** Accurate hours, address, phone number, categories, services, photos and a short, clear description. Keep it updated — especially hours on holidays.
- **Keep your details consistent** everywhere they appear: website, social profiles, directories.
- **Have a simple website** that loads quickly on a phone and answers the basics: what you do, where you are, prices or price ranges if possible, and how to book or contact you.

## 2. Be easy to trust

Local customers are choosing someone they'll often meet in person, and trust carries a lot of weight.

- **Ask happy customers for reviews**, consistently and politely — a short message after a good experience works well. Reply to reviews, including the critical ones, calmly and helpfully.
- **Show real people and real work.** Photos of your team, your space and your results build more trust than stock images ever will.
- **Be specific.** "Physiotherapy for sports injuries and back pain, with evening appointments" is more trustworthy than "complete wellness solutions".

## 3. Be easy to choose

Many local businesses lose customers at the final step.

- **Make booking or enquiring effortless.** Online booking, WhatsApp, a clear phone number — whatever your customers prefer.
- **Respond fast.** A same-hour reply often wins the customer.
- **Offer a clear first step** — a consultation, a first visit, a trial class — that lowers the commitment.

## Choose one channel to build on

Beyond the basics, choose *one* channel to build consistently for six months rather than trying everything:

- **Instagram or short video** if your work is visual (food, beauty, fitness, interiors).
- **Local search and content** if people search for your service when they have a specific need (clinics, repairs, professional services).
- **Community and partnerships** if trust and referrals drive your category (tutors, wellness, specialist services).

## Retention is marketing too

For most local businesses, existing customers are the most valuable source of growth. A simple way to stay in touch — a WhatsApp broadcast list, an occasional email, a loyalty offer — often outperforms chasing new customers.

## A monthly rhythm

Keep it light and consistent:

- **Weekly:** reply to reviews and messages; post or publish once or twice.
- **Monthly:** check where new customers came from (just ask them); update photos and offers; review what worked.
- **Quarterly:** step back and ask whether your message still matches what customers value most.

Local marketing rewards consistency far more than cleverness. Show up reliably in the places nearby customers look, make it easy to trust and choose you, and the compounding effect over a year can be significant.
