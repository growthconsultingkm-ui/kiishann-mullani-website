Marketing problems don't usually arrive as a single dramatic failure. They show up as a collection of quieter symptoms that are easy to explain away individually. Here are seven worth taking seriously — and what each one usually points to.

## 1. Your best months are unpredictable

If you can't explain why one month was great and the next was slow, you probably don't have a reliable system for generating demand. Growth that depends on luck, referrals you can't influence or one big client is fragile.

**What it usually points to:** no consistent, repeatable source of new customers.

## 2. Customers can't explain what makes you different

Ask a few recent customers why they chose you. If the answers are vague — "you seemed good", "a friend mentioned you" — your positioning isn't doing its job. Referrals are wonderful, but they shouldn't be the only reason people find and choose you.

**What it usually points to:** unclear positioning and messaging.

## 3. You compete mainly on price

When customers see little difference between you and the alternatives, price becomes the deciding factor. Constant discounting and price objections are often a marketing problem disguised as a pricing problem.

**What it usually points to:** a weak value story and not enough proof.

## 4. You're active on many channels, effective on none

Posting a little everywhere feels responsible. In practice, spreading thin usually means nothing gets enough attention to work. It's better to do two channels properly than six half-heartedly.

**What it usually points to:** channel choices made without a strategy.

## 5. Leads arrive, but the wrong ones

If much of your sales time is spent with people who can't afford you, don't need you or aren't ready, your marketing is attracting the wrong audience — or failing to filter.

**What it usually points to:** unclear targeting and messages that don't qualify.

## 6. Nobody owns marketing

When marketing is something everyone does "when there's time", it gets done inconsistently. Someone needs to own the plan, the budget and the results, even in a small business.

**What it usually points to:** no clear ownership or rhythm.

## 7. You can't say what's working

If you don't know where your best customers came from, you can't do more of what works. You don't need complex attribution — just a habit of asking customers how they found you and tracking it.

**What it usually points to:** no measurement, even a simple one.

## What to do if several of these sound familiar

Don't try to fix everything at once. Start with the foundations, in this order:

1. **Clarify who you're for and why they should choose you.**
2. **Pick one or two channels** where those customers actually pay attention.
3. **Assign ownership** and a simple monthly review.
4. **Track where customers come from**, even if it's just a question on your enquiry form.

Most marketing problems are solvable. The first step is to stop treating the symptoms one at a time and look at the system underneath.
